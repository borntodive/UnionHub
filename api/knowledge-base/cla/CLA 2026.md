# 1. BASIC PRINCIPLES OF THE COLLECTIVE LABOUR AGREEMENT

The parties (ANPAC/FIT-CISL and Malta Air) agree the following:

1.1 This agreement will be governed by Italian law and the courts of Italy will have jurisdiction over any disputes arising from this collective agreement.

1.2 The employment of all directly employed Malta Air Pilots based in Italy including Mobile pilots employed in Italy, will be governed by Italian law.

1.3 The payroll structure agreed will continue as specifically indicated hereunder for the duration of the agreement but must at all times comply with the Italian tax code and any amendments to same including any rulings by the Italian tax authorities.

1.4 The parties agree that the pay outlined in this agreement will be the only material changes to Italian pilot pay and allowances for the duration of this agreement. Therefore, they agree as well that there will be no further pay increasing claims or other claims whatsoever regarding the pay during the term of this agreement.

1.5 The agreement will cover all Italian based Malta Air Employed Pilots including those with a contract "Mobile" for all aircraft types and variants including any new Italian airport bases opened throughout the duration of this CLA.

1.6 ANPAC/FIT-CISL acknowledge that Malta Air is a highly efficient low-cost airline whose success depends on offering customers the lowest fares and using a highly efficient business model.

1.7 ANPAC/FIT-CISL and Malta Air agree to commence negotiations on a new collective agreement by 31 Mar 2030 at least 12 months prior to the end of this agreement which expires on 31 March 2031. ANPAC/FIT-CISL Malta Air agree to maintain Malta Air's industry leading fixed 5/4 roster pattern in Italy for the duration of this agreement. In circumstances where negotiations are ongoing regarding a new CLA after 31 March 2031 but have not been concluded by the expiry date of this CLA, all parties agree to extend the terms of this CLA for a maximum of 12 months to finalise the terms of a new CLA.

1.8 All the amounts indicated in the agreement are gross, unless otherwise specified.

1.9 The Collective Labor Agreement will also cover:

- Employment contract provisions.
- Rostering, part-time, annual leave and out of base guidelines.
- Maintaining Malta Air's level of pilot productivity.
- Health and Safety management and Just Culture protocols.

  1.10 The company is making continuous investments in new systems to improve pilots' working lives. References in this document to specific computer systems, including Zendesk, webexpenses, Ryanair Connect etc, reflect the IT Tools currently in use and shall not restrict the roll out of replacements for these systems or new technological tools. During the regular operational engagements, Malta Air will update ANPAC/Fit-Cisl on any significant new and coming system changes.

## 3. SALARY &amp; PAY STRUCTURE

### 3.1 TFR &amp; Sanivolo Contributions

3.1.1 The TFR will be calculated based on pilots' Basic Pay, Guaranteed Minimum Flight Allowance (GMFA), applicable training role allowances and will be divided by 13.5 to calculate the TFR amount to be paid as per art. 2120 of the Italian Civil Code. This amount will then be paid to the Fondaereo (Fondo Pensione Complementare Naviganti del Trasporto Aereo) on a monthly basis.

3.1.2 Malta Air will continue to make monthly contributions on behalf of individual pilots to the Italian aircrew public pension fund (Fondo Volo).

3.1.3 Where individual pilots wish to make additional contributions to Fondaereo, they should inform via Zendesk query the Malta Air Payroll department, who will arrange for the administration of the additional monthly contributions to Fondaereo through the Malta Air payroll but at no additional cost to Malta Air.

3.1.4 Pilots may opt to receive, on top of the TFR contribution, an additional annual Company matching contribution to their Fondaereo account of 2% of applicable earnings (basic salary, Guaranteed Minimum Flight Allowance (GMFA) and Flight Role Allowances), provided that the Pilots makes at least an equal contribution which will be processed as a deduction via the monthly payroll. This means that Malta Air will not make any contribution on behalf of Pilots who do not make any contribution themselves, or who make a contribution of less than 2% of their applicable earnings.

3.1.5 Starting from 01 Jul 2026, within 60 days of the employment date, an account in Fondaereo will be automatically created, unless otherwise requested by the new pilot.

3.1.6 Contributions will be made to the Cassa di Assistenza Sanitaria dei Piloti – Sanivolo for the administrative expenses of the Sanivolo fund for the duration of this agreement. From the 2026 yearly renewal of the insurance, the total maximum annual contribution will be €2,016 (inclusive of the 0.8% for administrative expenses).

### 3.2 Salary

A detailed breakdown of Pilot pay elements can be found in Appendix 1 – Pay Tables. The parties agree that effective 01 Apr 2027 Captain pay is based on years of service as a Captain within the Ryanair Group. Detail on the application of these service groups is included in Note 2 of Appendix 1 – Pay Tables.

3.2.1 Basic Pay – Pilots annual basic pay (only) will be paid in 13 monthly instalments, with the twelfth and thirteenth installments paid in December each year. The pro-rata payment of the 13th-month instalment will be made to all Malta Air pilots who, during the year, are transferred, dismissed, or resign, in proportion to the amount accrued up to their last day of service under this Malta Air contract. The payment will be included in the last available pay slip.

3.2.2 Guaranteed Minimum Flight Allowance (GMFA) – This shall be paid in 12 monthly instalments.

3.2.3 Hourly Flight Allowance (HFA) – This shall be paid for each scheduled block hour actually operated. This means the planned (scheduled) flight duration as set out in the Pilot's published roster for flights actually flown by the pilot.

3.2.4 Diaria (DIA) – A daily amount of €46.48 shall be payable in respect of any duty performed away from the home base, including but not limited to positioning (Deadheading), standby duties, office work, training, or simulator services.

3.2.5 Home Base Duty (HBD) – When pilots are on standby, office duty or engaged in training or simulator services within their home base, they shall receive a gross daily amount of €46.55.

3

3.2.5.1 Sickness Payment (SICK) - Where a pilot is unavailable for work due to illness, pilots will be paid a supplementary daily rate of €46.55. Payment of the supplementary rate for absence due to illness will only be made up to a maximum of 120 days or 6 calendar months (whichever comes first) in any 3-year period and will be counted as part of the 190-day maximum payable in each calendar year as per 3.2.7 below. The supplementary daily rate (€46.55) will not be included in the TFR calculation. Should Italian legislation amendments improve the INPS sickness payment, the parties will meet to discuss its implementation while maintaining the principle that pilots will receive at least this SICK payment (including any INPS payments). In the event of the introduction of INPS payments in excess of this SICK payment, the INPS scheme will replace this SICK scheme.

3.2.6 Annual Cap: The payment of €46,48 for diaria and €46.55 for Home Base Duties (including absences due to SICK) is capped at a maximum of 190 days in any calendar year i.e. the absolute maximum amount to be paid, including sick days, will be €8,844.50 per pilot in any calendar year.

3.2.7 Annual Leave Allowance (ALA) – For each day of annual leave, a gross flight allowance shall be paid as additional compensation for flight hours not flown. Details of such compensation included in Appendix 1 – Pay Tables.

3.2.8 Working Day Off (WOFF) – This is a day when the pilot commences a flight duty period operating commercial flights (or a Ferry flight operated as Captain or Co-Pilot) at the Company's request on a rostered day off. For such duties an additional flight allowance shall be paid at a fixed gross rate of €900 for Captains, €450 for First Officers and Junior First Officers, and €225 for Second Officers.

3.2.9 Out of Base Overnight Allowance (OOB) – The overnight off-base allowance is set at €75 net per night for all roles and ranks. The gross-to-net conversion is detailed in Appendix 1 - Pay Tables.

3.2.10 Flight Additional Indemnity (FAI) – This is a component of the Guaranteed Minimum Flight Allowance (GMFA) which is worth €8,000 for Captain and €7,500 for Copilot designed to cover expenses directly related to flight duties (as health insurance, loss of license, uniform/ID, SIM expenses, car parking, medical, water &amp; phone). As these expenses arise solely from active flying duties originating from the home base, this allowance is reflected in the Guaranteed Minimum Flight Allowance (GMFA) in the pay slips.

3.2.11 Flight Role Allowance (FRA) – This is an additional flight pay paid to all Base Captain (BC), Type Rating Examiner (TRE), Type Rating Instructor (TRI), Line Training Captain (LTC), Standards Line Training Captain (LTCS), Line Check Captain (LCC) and Synthetic Flight Instructor (SFI) for their flight activity.

3.2.12 Payroll Withholding Taxes – Malta Air will apply payroll withholding taxes to all payments made under this agreement in accordance with applicable tax laws (including Court Rulings and Tax Authority Guidelines) that are applicable at any given time.

3.2.13 New Captains (Direct Entry) or command upgrades to an Italian base will be paid 90% of the applicable Captain pay rates (all pay elements excluding diaria, home base duty and FAI) for the first 12 months of their Captain contract. The GMFA will be paid at 90% of the applicable rate plus €800 p.a. to take account of the FAI.

3.2.14 Special Duty Paid (ISD) – Miscellaneous company required and rostered special duty with additional pay.

3.2.15 Ground School (IGS) – Rostered activities for lessons carried out on the ground by the instructor/examiner.

3.2.16 Test Flight (TF) – Rostered activity carried out in flight to test the aircraft.

3.2.17 Hourly Flight Allowance SIM (HFA SIM) – Daily payment for rostered activity carried out in the simulator.

3.2.18 Statutory Leave Divisor – Malta Air will apply a 26 day divisor for statutory leave deductions.

## 5.4 PART-TIME

Part time roster availability is decided upon by the Company based on operational requirements/time of the year. At least once per year, the Company will advertise any requirement that may arise for part time roster arrangements. This advertisement will typically be issued in advance of 15 October each year. Any additional requirements for part time arrangements based upon operational needs will be advertised as it arises through the year.

The types of part time roster which may become available are:

A. Standard 50% - 5 days ON, 13 days OFF or 4 days ON, 10 days OFF. As has always been the case, year-round arrangements include three full-time peak summer months as per individual agreements.

B. Full year 50% - 5 days on, 13 days OFF.

C. Winter 50% (5 months – Nov to Mar) 5 days ON, 13 days OFF or 4 days ON, 10 days OFF roster.

D. Yearly 75% (9 months working – 3 months off) 5 days ON, 4 days OFF from 16 Feb to 14 Nov and OFF for 3 months from 15 Nov to 15 Feb.

E. Year-round or Winter 66% (5 months Nov to Mar) 5 days ON, 13 days OFF, 5 days ON, 4 days OFF

When a pilot who is on any part time pattern voluntarily agrees to operate flight duties on a rostered day OFF, that day will trigger a WOFF payment as per 3.2.9 plus all the relevant variable salary elements.

The parties agree that available part time rosters by base and rank will be granted in order of priority as outlined below:

1. Pilots with verified Law 104 status.

2. New mothers who completed their maternity leave in the prior 6 months.

3. Parents with children under the age of 13.

4. All other Pilots per seniority (DOE), in the case of same DOE, the older pilot will have priority.

Pilots on part time rosters will receive all the fixed pay elements paid proportionally to the part-time percentage (i.e. payment will be proportionate to roster percentage, so in the case of a 50% roster pattern the pilot will be paid 50% of the fixed elements) and 100% of the variable sector pay rate for the months during which they are on part time. A/L entitlements will be adjusted accordingly.

## 5.5 PILOT PRODUCTIVITY

5.5.1 Both parties agree the application of this CLA guarantees no loss of productivity which enables Malta Air to plan and execute its flying program with a crewing ratio of no more than 5.4 crews per aircraft.

5.5.2 Command and other non-recurrent training (i.e. conversion training, LTC, TRI/E training etc.) will be rostered on the basis of 5 on 2 off or whatever pattern is necessary to expedite training, subject to Malta Air Ops Manual, Part A, Chapter 7.

5.5.3 Annual Leave and roster patterns are subject to change on promotion or transfer but will be maintained where possible to ensure Pilot's planned leave is not disrupted unnecessarily. However, Malta Air's operation must remain efficient to sustain our business model which requires a balance of roster patterns and in certain circumstances may require changes to pilot's patterns to ensure the balance of the roster and the efficiency of Malta Air's operation.

5.5.4 It is accepted that in exceptional circumstances if operational or industry circumstances change, (such as a heavy training burden, an aircraft introduction, or above normal levels of resignations/recruitment, safety events or force majeure), then the integrity of revenue flight operations will remain paramount and following consultation with ANPAC/FIT-CISL the agreed rostering guidelines may be suspended or altered for periods of 28 days at a time and the basic provisions of the Malta Air Ops Manual, Part A, Chapter 7 will apply.

- Assess if the impasse or dispute can be re-evaluated and avoided
- Mediate if the situation requires it
- Evaluate alternatives, where applicable
- Refer to next level in the dispute resolution process, should that be required.

The first stage of the process can be initiated by either party through a formal letter addressed to the HR Manager for Malta Air or to the Pilot Committee, setting out the details of the "issue" or "impasse". As part of the first stage, within 5 working days following receipt of the aforementioned letter detailing the "issue" or "impasse", Malta Air will arrange a formal meeting in order to attempt to resolve the dispute. This meeting will take place within 14 calendar days from the date of receipt of the first letter.

B. In case of failure to agree at the first stage, a second stage of the dispute resolution process must be formally initiated.

The second stage meeting shall take place within 14 calendar days following the conclusion of the first stage — the first stage will have deemed to have concluded if either party advises (the other party) in writing that the second stage of the dispute resolution process should be initiated (but only following a first stage meeting having taken place). In order to facilitate the resolution of the dispute, when required and considered appropriate by both parties the second stage may include the attendance of higher-level management compared to the first stage. The parties may request the level of management appropriate to the second stage meeting and the other party will endeavor to accommodate such request. The second stage may not be blocked by either party in the case that certain levels of management cannot attend the second stage of the dispute resolution process for any reason.

C. If either party is unavailable to attend a meeting as per the deadlines outlined in points 1 and 2, the meetings will be rescheduled at the earliest opportunity and no later than 10 working days from the date initially proposed. The parties may agree to extend the above timelines whenever this is considered useful to facilitate a resolution of the "issue" or "impasse". It is intended that all communication will be exchanged between ANPAC /FIT-CISL and the Malta Air Dublin Office. Industrial action declaration process shall be started by ANPAC/FIT-CISL only following the conclusion, without success, of both formal stages of the process outlined above.

# Appendix 3-A: MALTA AIR (ITALY) ANNUAL LEAVE SYSTEM

## 1. Objectives

To deliver an annual leave system which:

1.1 Ensures fairness and transparency when allocating annual leave, especially during peak periods when little annual leave is available.

1.2 Provides a simple application process that is easy for pilots to understand and for the company to administer.

1.3 Recognizes pilots' desire to have some leave accommodated at peak periods to try to facilitate pilots' personal preferences.

1.4 Maintains productivity, i.e. no reduction in days available for flying, and no requirement to increase crewing levels.

1.5 Protects the operation to ensure sufficient pilots are available to operate the full flying and training program in each base.

1.6 Ensures that annual leave applications are made and processed well in advance to prevent any risk to the operation from annual leave accumulating.

1.7 Maintains open, clear, and rapid communications with pilots to assist pilots with planning their leave.

## 2. General Principles

2.1 Directly employed Italy based pilots will receive an annual leave entitlement per calendar year (01 Jan - 31 Dec) of 20 annual leave days. They will also be entitled to 9 public holidays that Pilots could normally work based on the 5 on 4 off roster. This fully accounts for the Italian statutory leave entitlement.

2.2 For ease of processing and to assist pilots with planning, annual leave will be split into two types, blocks of 5 days ("block leave") and single leave days ("ad-hoc leave").

2.3 Block leave will consist of 4 blocks of 5 days (20 days in total)

2.4 Ad-hoc leave will consist of: (9 days in total).

1. Eight days ad-hoc leave to be applied for (of which 2 days will be "priority days").

2. One day of ad-hoc leave will be allocated on Christmas day.

2.5 Pilots apply for annual leave via the "Ryanair Connect" app.

2.6 With a view to accommodating as many pilots' annual leave preferences as possible, Pilots within a base may be required to temporarily switch from early to late duties (or vice versa) to ensure sufficient coverage is available to accommodate colleagues' leave. At all times such switches will be temporary, FTL compliant and only for the purpose of accommodating annual leave.

2.7 The company will allow for wedding priority where annual leave will be allocated — once in a pilot's career at Malta Air — for their own wedding at least 12 months in advance and with priority over all other annual leave in the base. Pilots granted such leave in a given year will change to the lowest priority leave group in the following year in accordance with section 3 below.

2.8 Pilots working part-time will have their leave entitlement reduced proportionately to the part-time percentage

3.3 This list by rank will be divided into three groups; A, B and C with the most senior pilot assigned to A, the second most senior to B, third to C, fourth to A, fifth to B, sixth to C and so on. The following order of priority will then apply for assessing leave applications:

# 4. Applications for Block Leave (4 blocks of 5 days)

4.1 Block leave applications will be run in two application window phases (Phase 1 and Phase 2) to allow pilots whose requested leave cannot be allocated in Phase 1 to apply for alternative dates.

4.2 Block leave application window Phase 1 for the next calendar year will open 15 Aug and close 15 Sep annually. All applications will be processed and responded to by 1 Oct.

4.3 Block leave application window Phase 2 will open 2 Oct and close 16 Oct annually. All applications will be processed and responded to by 30 Oct.

4.4 A block consists of 5 consecutive working days in between 2 blocks of off days.

4.5 Pilots must apply for a minimum of 2 blocks (which can be consecutive) in the winter schedule (Nov - Mar inclusive).

4.6 Pilots can apply for a maximum of 2 blocks in the summer schedule (Apr - Oct inclusive), but preference will be given to applications for single blocks of five days which equates to 13 calendar days free of duty.

4.7 Malta Air have implemented a new system, which will deliver at least 1 block of annual leave during the period 1 Jun – 30 Sept (this excludes pilots undergoing training or operating of secondments outside of Italy).

4.8 If a block of 5 days straddles two seasons, it will be counted as belonging to the season in which at least 3 working days fall.

4.9 During each Phase, pilots may apply for up to 10 preferences for their block leave with each preference including the pilot's proposed dates for each of the four blocks. If any element of the first preference cannot be accommodated, then the rostering department will move on to the second preference and so on.

4.10 Pilots will be allowed to add freeform text to their application to assist the rostering team in accommodating their preferences (e.g. "I just need any block in Sep or Oct").

4.11 All 4 blocks must be applied for in Phase 1.

4.12 Any outstanding block days entitlement must be applied for in block Phase 2.

4.13 Pilots who have applied for blocks in accordance with the guidelines but have blocks rejected and still have block leave outstanding, will be contacted individually by pilot rostering who will attempt to try accommodating the pilots' preferences for leave before it is allocated on the roster.

# Appendix 3-B: MALTA AIR (ITALY) PILOT BASE TRANSFER SYSTEM

The base transfer selection criteria is based on a system hybrid of date-of-entry (DOE) and date-of-application (DOA) in accordance with the terms set out in this agreement. This agreement applies to bases in Italy only. Malta Air has negotiated similar agreements with other unions to cover other national jurisdictions.

The company will operate this system on Base Transfers in the normal course, as the common objective is to get every pilot to the base they want for the long term, in an orderly and transparent way, driven by a hybrid system of seniority with the agreed date-of-entry as first day of ground-school and actual date of application for a base at the current rank.

However, during the growth phase of the airline and given the multiplicity of bases, there will be exceptional circumstances during this period (such as a heavy training burden, an aircraft introduction, abnormal levels of resignations/recruitment or force majeure) which could require the suspension of how the base transfer system driven by seniority will operate, so the airline can continue to operate, grow and manage its business. ANPAC/FIT-CISL will be consulted regarding any such suspension and the reasons it arises. This agreement cannot be used to impede the growth of Malta Air and the ability of the company to operate its base structure and resource the required crewing ratios in order to operate its schedule for its customers.

## 1. Objectives

To deliver a Base Transfer System that:

1.1 Helps achieve the Company's goal of getting all pilots to their preferred base.

1.2 Recognizes that base transfers are very important for our pilots and their family life.

1.3 Is fair and transparent.

1.4 Takes account of a pilot's seniority and date of application in transfer decisions.

1.5 Provides pilots with relevant and up-to-date information by way of the base preference system on "Ryanair Connect"

1.6 Does not impinge upon the Company's operation and growth plans

1.7 Is easy to understand for pilots and is easy to manage by the company.

## 2. General Principles

2.1 This agreement covers base transfer applications (including new base openings) and transfers required in cases of base closures / downsizing / crewing reductions.

2.2 Base transfers will be granted by a hybrid system of date-of-entry and date of application subject to the process and requirements set out in this document. Date-of-entry will be defined as first day of ground school in the Ryanair Group (whether directly employed or contractor).

2.3 Effective 1 Nov 2026, the base transfer lists will be sorted based on the following formula:

A. (days since date-of-entry × 0.6) + (days since date of application × 0.4)

2.4 Date-of-application will be defined as the first date the pilot joined the waiting list for their rank at that particular base. This applies to a first or second preference. Pilots who were appointed as SFIs, will have the total calendar days spent appointed as an SFI added to the "days since date of application" for the purpose of the formula at 2.3 above.

2.5 During each ground school training course (typically 12 pilots, who will all have the same date-of-entry), a lottery will be conducted to randomly rank those pilots (e.g. from 1 to 12). If two or more pilots have the same date-of-entry, the ground school lottery number will be the deciding factor whereby the pilot with the lowest lottery number will be granted the transfer.

2.6 Successful command upgrade Captains must complete a minimum of 12 months in their agreed Command Upgrade base before being considered for another base transfer. This is necessary to provide stability in the airline's operations.

2.7 At all times Malta Air must maintain the right to determine the appropriate crewing and experience levels, what crewing needs exist and whether those needs will be met by transfer pilots, promotions, newly recruited (i.e. direct entry pilots) or mobile pilots. This provision is necessary to ensure an appropriate spread of experience levels and training resources are in place to maintain adequate crewing as the company grows.

2.8 From 1 Feb 2027 lists will be resorted on 1 Feb and 1 Sep annually based on the above formula at 2.3

## 3. Italy Base Opening / Closure / Downsizing / Crewing Reductions

3.1 Base Openings: in the event of a new base opening in Italy, the Company will advertise positions at that base within 14 days of the base announcement and for a minimum period of 14 days. The positions in the new base will be filled by date-of-entry from the pilots who applied during the base opening application window.

3.2 Downsizing: in the event of downsizing (i.e. where the company determines that crewing levels need to be reduced) or a base closure, the Company will meet with ANPAC/Fit-Cisl to brief them on the required headcount changes and will then (subject to the applicable law), in order of priority:

- Seek volunteers for transfers to alternative bases.
- Process any required compulsory transfers. This will be processed according to the criteria identified in section 3.3 below.

24

3.3 In case of compulsory transfers, the below process will be applied:

3.3.1 Any Pilot selected for a contractual transfer will receive at least 30 calendar days' notice in writing of their selection, of their new base, and start date.

3.3.2 A point-system based upon objective criteria will be used to determine the Pilots selected for contractual transfers. The Pilots selected for contractual transfers will be those with lowest points. Points will be assigned as follows:

A. 6 points: will be awarded for each 'dependent minor' up to the age of 14 years.
B. 3 points: will be awarded for each 'dependent' aged between 15 and 21 years.
C. Dependent Points Cap: the combined maximum points awarded to any pilot with respect of sections 3.3.2A and 3.3.2B above will be 15 points.
D. 1 point: will be awarded for each completed month of service with the Ryanair Group (calculated using ground school entry).
E. Tie-Break: in case of equal scores, priority will be giving to the pilot with the higher (longer) 'date-in-base'.
F. Law 104/1992: Pilots who qualify as carers under the provisions of Law 104/1992 will automatically be placed at the bottom of any selection list - i.e., will be the last Pilots of their rank to be selected for contractual transfers.

3.3.3 In the event that multiple Pilots have selected the same basing alternative, and demand for positions outweighs supply, positions will be offered based on the selection criteria set out above (Section 3.3.2)

3.3.4 All Ryanair Group contracts allow for transfers with no additional payment. In the event of a base closure, the parties will meet to consider the appropriateness of other measures in the circumstances, including additional time off for pilots to facilitate the base move and a contribution towards moving expenses.

3.4 Pilots who are transferring due to a base closure, will be offered first priority over all available positions at the time that applications are being processed based on their seniority.

3.5 Sections 3.2 and 3.3 above will address transfers arising as a result of base closures, downsizing or crewing reductions where jobs are preserved. Sections 3.2 and 3.3 are not an agreement on the selection criteria to be applied if redundancies were to arise, in which case the required redundancy consultation would take place.

3.6 In the event that a base closure or downsizing is reversed within two years and transfer positions become available, any pilots who transferred out as a result of the closure / downsizing will be given priority to return to that base above any others on the waiting list.

## 4. Italy Direct Entry Pilot (DEP) Recruitment

## 6. Italy Transfer Application Process

## 7. Italy Transfer Offer Process

It is essential that the necessary controls are maintained within the transfer offer process to ensure that Pilots do not submit frequent and/or frivolous base transfer applications. 7.4 If a pilot refuses or fails to accept a transfer offer to their primary base preference, the application is deleted, and they will not be eligible for another base transfer within the following 12 months unless a shorter timeframe is agreed by the company.

7.5 If a pilot refuses or fails to accept a transfer offer to their secondary base preference, the secondary base preference application is deleted and they will not be eligible for a base transfer to that base for at least 6 months, but the primary base preference application is unaffected.

# Appendix 3-C: MALTA AIR (ITALY) COMMAND UPGRADE PROCESS

## How Seniority Applies to Command Upgrade

In circumstances where there are more command upgrade applicants than positions available across the network, the following will apply:

1. Command Upgrade positions will be advertised. Candidates who achieve satisfactory training assessments, complete the route-to-command phases, score two consecutive good or better grades in their recurrent simulator sessions, have a satisfactory attendance record and receive a satisfactory grading on coaching flights will be put forward for a Command upgrade assessment based on seniority under the Italian Seniority List (ISL).

2. A Command Upgrade assessment will include elements as specified by the Flight Operations Head of Training or as specified by EASA and the National Aviation Authority as detailed below.

3. Candidates successful at Command Upgrade assessment will be placed on the next available Command course based on their seniority under the ISL.

## 1. Basing

A Co-Pilot's promotion to Captain will be at an agreed designated base. The candidate will be offered options for basing at airports based on operational requirements. Initial basing after command upgrade will last for a minimum period of 12 months. The basing will be agreed and confirmed to the command upgrade candidate before the ground school commences. Before deciding on their command upgrade base, Co-Pilots can receive on their request a copy of the draft contract for that base. A Co-Pilot who refuses an offer of a position on a Command Upgrade course will not be considered for future courses for at least 18 months.

## 2. Command Upgrade Training

Candidates must successfully complete all aspects of the training course to be appointed as a commander by the Chief Pilot. Additional training may be provided at the company's discretion. In the event that a candidate fails the training course he or she will be returned to the line as a First Officer.

Any placement on a future command upgrade course will be at the discretion of the Chief Pilot. Head of Training or their designated deputy.

## 3. Contract

Co-Pilots who successfully complete all aspects of the command training program will be issued with a contract of employment as a Captain in the designated base that was agreed between the Co-Pilot and the Command Upgrade coordinator prior to the command upgrade course.

## 4. Command Appointment

Upon successful completion of the Command Upgrade course and associated Line Training and signing the command contract, Co-Pilots are appointed to Captain by the Chief Pilot.

## 5. Transfers

After 12 months in the Command Upgrade base, the Pilot can be considered for a transfer using his/her relative seniority in accordance with the base transfer system.

## 6. Repayment of training costs

The cost of the Command Upgrade Training to include Ground school, Simulator sessions and line training is €6,500. Co-Pilots entering the command course will be required to sign an agreement on the repayment of training costs. The terms of this agreement will require the co-pilot to repay the cost of the command upgrade training if he or she ceases operating on Malta Air aircraft within two years of completion of the command course.

## 7. Initial 100 Hours

Newly appointed Captains are subject to operational safety restrictions for their first 100 flying hours. The new Captain may have to operate at another base (separate to their agreed designated base for 12 months) for the first 100 hours. If that is a third base (i.e. not their FO base or CU base), then accommodation and Out of base overnight allowance expenses will be provided for the rostered duties.

## 8. Trial Period and Line check

Initial command upgrade appointments will be subject to a 6-month trial period during which the appointee's performance as a Captain will be closely monitored. A review as to his/her suitability for continued command will take place at the end of that six-month period and in the unlikely event that such a review is unsuccessful, the pilot will be returned to the rank of First Officer. The cost of the Command Upgrade training course will not have to be repaid if the candidate fails the six-month trial period.

## 9. Amendments to this Process

The Company reserves the right to alter or amend the Command Upgrade assessment or training process to meet the operational needs of the business and any regulatory requirement. This won't affect Malta Air's agreement to allocate Command Upgrade assessment slots based on seniority when the Company's growth slows as set out above. While Malta Air will adhere to the terms set out under this agreement, ANPAC/FIT-CISL recognize that Malta Air is experiencing a period of growth within Italy and this agreement must not:

A. Impede, restrict, or prevent the efficient operation of Malta Air flight schedules.

B. Impede, restrict, or prevent the airline's ability to assign duties to run the schedule in accordance with individual contracts of employment and the Ops manual.

C. Impede, restrict, or prevent flexibility on crewing levels necessary to deliver the airline's growth.
